About the Krell Patch Topology

In a self-cycling Krell patch, the synthesizer governs its own timing without an external clock or sequencer:

  • Self-Cycling Slope: A function generator cycles through rise and fall phases. Completing the fall phase triggers random sample-and-hold stages.
  • Dynamic Parameter Selection: Each cycle samples a new oscillator pitch and updates the rise and fall durations for the subsequent cycle.
  • Low-Pass Gate: The signal routes through a 292-inspired LPG operating in Both mode (simultaneous variable low-pass filtering and attenuation). The control envelope modulates gate openness; the heuristic vactrol model imparts characteristic asymmetric optocoupler decay.
  • ARP MODAMP (Balanced Modulator Model): A functional model inspired by the ARP 2500 Model 1005 ModAmp (4014 schematic and 1005 MODAMP description). The 4014/1005 is a balanced modulator rather than reverb hardware. Here it performs pure bipolar signal multiplication with a sine carrier oscillator (10–1000 Hz) before reverb, producing sum and difference sidebands across the entire voice without dry blending. This is a functional signal multiplication model, not a transistor-exact circuit simulation.
  • Spring Reverb (Dispersive Tank Model): Positioned at the end of the musical processing chain as the final sound-shaping stage before oscilloscope analysis, stereo panning, and master level control. Serial synthetic dispersive delay network with chirped dispersion and metallic reflection; not a sampled impulse response or physical spring emulation.
